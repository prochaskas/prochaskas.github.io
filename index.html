Stephen Prochaska

PhD student, Information School, University of Washington

I study how rumors and disinformation take shape online, especially around U.S. elections: how people collectively make sense of uncertain events, how participatory disinformation draws on deeper stories people already hold, and how “evidence” gets produced and framed as events unfold.

I’m a PhD student in Information Science at the University of Washington, advised by Kate Starbird and Jevin West, and a researcher with the Center for an Informed Public. My work pairs qualitative, interpretive analysis with computational methods, from temporal semantic clustering to agent-based simulation. During election seasons I contribute to the Center’s rapid-response rumor research, published in The Election Rumor Rundown.

Before my PhD I studied psychology at Beloit College and earned a master’s in Information Management at UW. I also write on my personal Substack, Imagining ImPossible Futures.
